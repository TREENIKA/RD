    Cyber careers

    Jobs that protect the digital world

    These salary ranges are rough U.S. estimates and can change over time and by location.

    Cybersecurity Analyst

    Typical salary: $70,000 – $120,000 per year

    • Watches networks for suspicious activity and alerts.
    • Investigates possible attacks and helps stop them.
    • Sets up tools that keep computers and data safe.

    Penetration Tester (Ethical Hacker)

    Typical salary: $80,000 – $140,000 per year

    • Gets paid to try to break into systems in a legal way.
    • Finds weaknesses before real hackers do.
    • Writes reports that show what to fix.

    Security Engineer

    Typical salary: $90,000 – $150,000 per year

    • Designs and builds secure systems and networks.
    • Chooses security tools like firewalls and encryption.
    • Works with developers to make apps safer.

    Incident Responder

    Typical salary: $75,000 – $130,000 per year

    • Acts quickly when an attack or data breach happens.
    • Figures out what went wrong and what was damaged.
    • Helps systems recover and prevents future attacks.

    Security Consultant

    Typical salary: $85,000 – $150,000 per year

    • Advises schools, companies, or governments on staying safe.
    • Reviews security plans and policies.
    • Trains people on how to avoid cyber risks.

    Digital Forensics Specialist

    Typical salary: $70,000 – $130,000 per year

    • Collects and studies digital evidence after a cybercrime.
    • Works with law enforcement or companies.
    • Helps explain what happened using data and logs.
    Where cyber is needed

    Public vs private sector

    Cybersecurity protects both private companies and public services. They work differently, but both are important.

    Private sector

    Businesses and organizations that sell products or services. They protect customers, money, and company data.

    Public sector

    Government and community services for everyone, paid for by taxes. They protect public safety and everyday life.

    Private sector

    Banks and Online Shopping

    • Guards your money, credit cards, and bank accounts.
    • Encrypts (scrambles) data when you buy things online.
    • Detects fake purchases and blocks stolen card use.
    Private sector

    Gaming, Social Media, and Apps

    • Protects player accounts, skins, and in‑game purchases.
    • Helps fight bullying, bots, and fake accounts.
    • Keeps photos, messages, and profiles safer from strangers.
    Private sector

    Healthcare Companies and Hospitals

    • Protects patient medical records and health information.
    • Keeps hospital machines and systems online.
    • Prevents scams that try to steal insurance or billing data.
    Public sector

    Schools and Colleges

    • Protects student grades, records, and personal info.
    • Stops hackers from shutting down online classes and tests.
    • Keeps school Wi‑Fi and devices safe for students and teachers.
    Public sector

    Government and Public Safety

    • Secures voting systems and important government data.
    • Helps protect military and national security systems.
    • Stops attacks on emergency services like 911 centers.
    Public sector

    Power, Water, and Transportation

    • Keeps electricity, water plants, and traffic systems running.
    • Protects trains, airports, and shipping from cyberattacks.
    • Guards sensors and smart devices that control equipment.

    Examples of who uses cybersecurity

    Private companies
    • Tech and cloud: Google, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon
    • Banks and payments: JPMorgan Chase, Visa, PayPal
    • Gaming and social media: Roblox, Epic Games, Xbox, PlayStation, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram
    • Health tech: hospital networks and health companies
    Public organizations
    • Government agencies: local, state, and national
    • Public schools and colleges: districts and universities
    • Public safety: emergency services and courts
    • Infrastructure: public utilities and transit systems
    Cybersecurity protects people, not just computers. Keep exploring, stay curious, and use your skills to help others stay safe online.
